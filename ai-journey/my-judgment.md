AI significantly improved my productivity. It helped me analyze requirements, explore alternatives, identify edge cases, and make decisions faster and with greater confidence. It was also useful for turning ideas into structured plans, implementation tasks, and tests.

However, AI should not be the final decision-maker. Its architectural suggestions can be more complex than the problem requires and may lead to over-engineering when business context, delivery constraints, or the expected lifetime of the system are unclear.

As the person responsible for the project, I must decide what is appropriate for the system today while keeping future evolution in mind. This means questioning AI suggestions, verifying generated code, and choosing the simplest design that satisfies the current requirements without blocking realistic future changes.

I see AI as a thinking partner and productivity tool—not a replacement for engineering judgment, domain knowledge, or ownership of the final outcome.
